The whole thing, in one paragraph

If you only read one thing on this page, read this.

Imagine a friend sells used iPhones. He doesn't make money because phones are cool. He makes money because he buys a phone for $200, cleans it up, and sells it for $400. The phone didn't change. The price did.

Real estate is that, with houses. You don't make money by owning a house. You make money because you bought it cheaper than it's worth — and because you already know, before you sign, who will buy or rent it.

In plain words: buy low, know your exit, don't fall in love with the house.

Two questions before any deal

  1. Am I paying less than it will be worth?
    If not, there's no deal. Nicer kitchen won't save it.
  2. Do I already know who takes it off my hands?
    A buyer, a renter, a room renter. A name, not a hope.
In plain words: if either answer is "I don't know", you don't have a deal — you have a wish.

One house, all the way through

Every example on this page uses this same house. Learn it once and the rest is repetition.

Our house

A tired 3-bed house in Boynton Beach

Today it's uglyold kitchen, bad floors, dated bathrooms
It needs about$50,000 of work
Fixed up, houses like it sell for$400,000
The owner wants$330,000
In plain words: this house is a $400,000 house wearing $50,000 of damage. That gap is where the money lives.
Word you'll hear

"ARV"

ARV = After Repair Value. It's just a fancy name for the price after the work is done.

Like this: the price of a car after you fix the dent. Our house's ARV is $400,000.
Word you'll hear

"Comps"

Short for comparables — three to five similar houses nearby that actually sold in the last few months. That's how you prove the ARV instead of guessing it.

Like this: checking what your old iPhone sells for on eBay before you price yours. Three real sales beat one opinion.

Your highest safe offer

This one number keeps you out of trouble. It's the most important thing on this page.

The work, in three steps:

  1. Take 70% of the fixed-up price.
    $400,000 × 0.70 = $280,000
  2. Subtract the repair cost.
    $280,000 − $50,000 = $230,000
  3. That's your ceiling. Offer less, never more.
Why 70%? The other 30% is not profit — it pays the closing costs, the months you own it, the surprises, and the profit you actually want. Skip it and you work for free.

Try it on any house

Highest safe offer

On the $330,000 asking price, this house is $100,000 too expensive. That's a negotiation, or a walk — not a deal.

The five ways to earn

Same house, five different businesses. This is what Santiago means by "salidas" — exits.

1 · Wholesale

You sell the paper, not the house

You sign a contract to buy the house cheap, then sell that contract to another investor for a fee. You never own anything.

You earn
a fee, called an assignment fee
You need
a small deposit, usually $1,000–$5,000
How long
2 to 8 weeks
Watch out
you need a buyer lined up, and the seller must allow it
Like this: you put a deposit on a concert ticket, then sell your spot in line for more. You never went to the show.
2 · Flip

You buy it, fix it, sell it

Buy at $230,000, spend $50,000, sell at $400,000.

You earn
the difference after all costs
You need
the most money of all five ways
How long
4 to 8 months
Watch out
repairs always cost more and take longer
The math that matters: every $10,000 you shave off the purchase price is $10,000 more profit. Nothing else moves the needle that hard.
3 · Buy and hold

You rent it and keep it

One family pays you rent every month. The house slowly becomes worth more.

You earn
monthly cash, plus value growth
You need
about 20–25% down from a bank
How long
years — this is the slow one
Watch out
empty months, repairs, insurance
Like this: buying a vending machine instead of selling snacks one by one.
4 · Coliving

You rent it by the room

Instead of one family paying $3,200, five people pay $950 each.

You earn
more rent from the same house
You need
furniture, about $3,000–$4,000 per room
How long
weeks to fill, then constant work
Watch out
the city may not allow it, and bills are on you
Like this: selling a pizza by the slice instead of whole. More money per pizza — but only if the slices sell.
The honest math: $57,000 a year by the room vs $38,400 to one family looks amazing. Subtract ~$12,000 of bills, furniture and cleaning, and you need 4 of the 5 rooms filled just to beat the family. Know that before you fall for the idea.
5 · Multifamily

A building with several units

A duplex, a 4-plex, a 20-unit building. Each unit has its own rent.

You earn
many rents at once
You need
the biggest down payment of all
How long
long hold; the value grows as rent grows
Watch out
bigger loans, bigger repairs
Like this: an apartment building is a small business that happens to be made of walls.

Every acronym and jargon word, decoded

These are the exact letters that get thrown at you. Nobody explains them. Here they are.

About the house and the price
ARV
After Repair Value
The price after the work is done."What the house is worth fixed up."
Comps
Comparables
Three to five nearby houses that recently sold, used to prove a price."The proof, not the guess."
PP
Purchase Price
What you agree to pay."The number everything starts from."
MAO
Maximum Allowable Offer
The most you can pay and still make money."My ceiling."
EMD
Earnest Money Deposit
The deposit that says I'm serious — held by a title company, never in your pocket."My good-faith money."
DOM
Days on Market
How long it's been for sale. A long time means the seller may be getting tired."How stale is this listing."
MLS
Multiple Listing Service
The main listing database agents use."The public menu of houses."
CMA
Comparative Market Analysis
An agent's report of what nearby houses sold for."Comps with a cover page."
REO
Real Estate Owned
A house the bank took back and is now selling."The bank's leftovers."
COE
Close of Escrow / Closing
The day the money changes hands and you own it."The finish line."
About the building and the money
NOI
Net Operating Income
What the building earns in a year after bills, before the loan payment."Profit before the bank."
ROI
Return on Investment
What you got back compared to what you put in. Put in $100, make $20 — that's 20%."How hard my money worked."
COC
Cash-on-Cash return
Like ROI, but only the cash that lands in your pocket each year vs the cash you put down."My yearly paycheck per dollar invested."
DSCR
Debt Service Coverage Ratio
Do the rents cover the loan payment? 1.20 means rent is 20% more than the payment."Does the building pay for itself?"
LTV
Loan-to-Value
The loan divided by the value. 75% LTV means the bank owns three quarters."How much of the house the bank owns."
PITI
Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance
The four pieces of a monthly mortgage payment."What I pay every month, all in."
DTI
Debt-to-Income
How much of your monthly income is already promised to other debts."How stretched I look to a bank."
Cap rate
Capitalization rate
The building's yearly profit divided by its price — a score for how hard it works."Its interest rate, kind of."
About the people and the rules
HOA
Homeowners Association
Neighborhood club with fees and rules — it can ban renting rooms."The private rulebook."
PM
Property Manager
The person who handles the tenants for you."My spare hands."
SFR
Single-Family Residence
A normal house for one household."One family, one house."
Turn
Turn / make-ready
Getting a unit ready for the next renter. That's the BH Rentals business."Between tenants, start to finish."

The rule that decides if coliving is even legal

Santiago wrote one line about this: "not every house can be run this way." Here's what it means where you live.

Palm Beach County, in simple terms

  • Up to 4 unrelated people in one house = a normal family. Legal.
  • 5 or more = the county treats it as a business, with permits and zoning rules.
  • The county only allows that in certain zones — usually not normal single-family streets.
  • Every sleeping room needs at least 70 square feet.
In plain words: four roommates is a family. Five is a business the county regulates.

And it's the city, not the county

Most addresses in Palm Beach County are inside a city — Boynton Beach, Delray, Boca, West Palm, Lake Worth, Wellington. Each one writes its own rules, and many are stricter.

In plain words: before anyone promises "we'll put 6 rooms in this house", you check the exact address with that city. One phone call saves a lawsuit.
This is studying, not legal advice. Before Black Harbour runs one coliving house, a Florida lawyer confirms the room count for that address in writing.

If you remember five things

  1. You get paid on the buy. Bad entry, bad deal — every time.
  2. Highest safe offer = 70% of the fixed price, minus repairs. $400,000 × .70 − $50,000 = $230,000.
  3. Five exits: wholesale (sell the contract), flip (sell the house), rent it, rent by the room, rent many units.
  4. Four unrelated people is a family in Palm Beach County. Five needs zoning.
  5. In Florida, insurance kills more deals than price. Get the quote early.
