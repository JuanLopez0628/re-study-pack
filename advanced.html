The rule that decides whether coliving is even legal

Santiago wrote it in one line: "No toda casa se puede operar de esta manera." Here is what that means in your market.

Palm Beach County (unincorporated)

Verified from the county's own zoning documents:

RuleWhat it says
FamilyUp to 4 unrelated people count as one household
Above 4Treated as a Congregate Living Facility — a different land use
Where CLF goesRM district + some PUDs, Class A conditional use permit
Sleeping roomMinimum 70 sq ft per single occupant

So a 5-room coliving house in single-family zoning is not automatically legal. Stay at 4, buy in multifamily zoning, or get the permit. Each has a price.

The city, not the county

Most PBC addresses sit inside a city with its own code: Boca Raton, Delray Beach, Boynton Beach, West Palm Beach, Lake Worth Beach, Riviera Beach, Wellington, Royal Palm Beach, Greenacres…

Before promising a room count, check the address for:

  • Unrelated-occupant limit
  • Short-term vs long-term rental rules
  • Parking requirements
  • Fire/safety, egress, detectors
  • Business tax receipt
  • HOA / deed restrictions
  • Insurance actually covering room rentals
This is study material, not legal advice. Before Black Harbour operates one coliving house, a Florida real estate attorney confirms the allowed room count for that exact address — in writing.

Guardrails — what you never do

The Florida line on wholesaling

Verified: you do not need a real estate license to assign your own purchase contract — you're a principal, not a broker.

You do cross the line the moment you market or negotiate someone else's property for a fee. That's unlicensed brokerage.

Earnest money lives in a title company or attorney escrow. Never your pocket.

The costs that quietly kill deals

Buy closing~1.5–2.5%
Sell closing~6–8% all-in
Doc stamps (deed)$0.70 per $100
Holdingtaxes + insurance + utilities / month
Hard money10–13% + 2–4 points
Taxes on fee incomeset aside 25–30%

In Florida, insurance is the number that kills more deals than price ever does. Quote it before you fall in love.

Questions to bring to the lawyer / CPA

  1. Which contract do we use, and does it allow assignment + a nominee?
  2. Who writes our room-rental leases, and are they compliant for each city we operate in?
  3. One LLC per deal, or one holding company?
  4. How is the assignment fee taxed — and do doc stamps apply to it?
  5. Which insurance policy actually covers room-by-room occupancy?
  6. For BH Rentals turns: what's our liability working inside someone else's house?
